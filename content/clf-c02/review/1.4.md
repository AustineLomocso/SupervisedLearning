# Understand concepts of cloud economics

This task covers why moving to AWS changes the financial picture of IT: how costs shift from up-front spending to usage-based billing, which costs disappear, and which habits keep cloud bills low. Expect scenario questions that ask you to pick the economic benefit, licensing option, or optimization practice that fits a stated need.

## Key concepts

### Fixed versus variable costs

On premises, a company makes a capital expense (CapEx): it buys servers, networking, and facilities before it knows its demand, and it pays for them whether they are busy or idle. AWS lets you trade that for variable expense (OpEx). You pay for what you consume, and you can stop paying when you stop using a resource. This lowers the risk of new projects, because there is no large investment to recover.

### Costs of on-premises environments

A fair comparison counts the total cost of ownership, not just server prices. On-premises costs include real estate, power and cooling, physical security, networking equipment, hardware purchase and refresh, software licenses, and the staff who operate all of it. Many of these are fixed and easy to overlook.

### Economies of scale and cost savings

AWS aggregates the usage of a very large number of customers, so it buys and operates infrastructure at a scale that individual companies rarely match. These savings are passed on as lower pay-as-you-go prices. You also stop guessing capacity: instead of buying for a predicted peak, you scale up and down as demand changes. Staff who once racked servers can work on projects that differentiate the business.

### Licensing strategies

Some software is offered with a **license included**, where the license fee is built into the hourly price (for example, Windows Server on Amazon EC2). With **Bring Your Own License (BYOL)**, you apply eligible licenses you already own to AWS resources, subject to the vendor's terms. BYOL avoids paying twice for software you have already bought; license-included is simpler and suits short-lived or variable workloads, or customers who own no licenses.

### Rightsizing

Rightsizing means matching instance types and sizes to measured workload needs. Servers copied from on-premises specifications are often larger than necessary. AWS Compute Optimizer analyzes utilization and recommends better-fitting sizes. Rightsizing is continuous, because demand changes.

### Automation

Automation, such as AWS CloudFormation templates or scheduled start and stop actions, makes deployments repeatable, reduces human error, saves staff time, and prevents idle resources from running. It supports the Cost Optimization pillar of the AWS Well-Architected Framework.

## Key terms

| Term                          | Meaning                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Capital expense               | Up-front spending on assets such as servers and data centers.                |
| Variable expense              | Spending that rises and falls with usage, as in pay-as-you-go pricing.       |
| Total cost of ownership (TCO) | All costs of running an environment, including facilities, power, and staff. |
| Economies of scale            | Lower unit costs from large volume, passed on to customers as lower prices.  |
| Rightsizing                   | Choosing resource sizes that match actual workload needs.                    |
| BYOL                          | Bring Your Own License: use licenses you already own on AWS.                 |
| License included              | The software license fee is part of the AWS usage price.                     |

## Comparisons

| Concept A                    | Concept B                     | Key difference                                                             |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fixed cost (capital expense) | Variable cost (pay as you go) | Paid before use and regardless of use, versus paid as consumed.            |
| BYOL                         | License included              | You supply the license, versus AWS bundles it into the price.              |
| Rightsizing                  | Automated scaling             | Best size for a steady load, versus capacity that follows changing demand. |
| AWS Compute Optimizer        | AWS Pricing Calculator        | Analyzes running resources, versus estimates planned usage.                |

## Exam tips

- "Trade fixed expense for variable expense" and "stop guessing capacity" are standard phrasings of the cloud economic benefits.
- Lower prices because of aggregate customer usage points to economies of scale.
- Over-provisioned instance with low utilization points to rightsizing and AWS Compute Optimizer.
- Existing licenses point to BYOL; no licenses or a bundled price points to license included.
- Do not choose answers that promise guaranteed savings, fixed costs, or the removal of all staff.
- Estimating cost before deployment points to AWS Pricing Calculator.
