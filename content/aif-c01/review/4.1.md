# Explain the development of AI systems that are responsible

Responsible AI means designing, building and operating AI so that it is fair, safe, accurate and trustworthy. This task covers the features of responsible AI, the tools that help you apply it, responsible model selection, legal risks of generative AI (GenAI), dataset quality, bias and variance, and ways to monitor bias and truthfulness. Expect scenario questions that ask you to match a risk or goal to the right concept or tool.

## Key concepts

### Features of responsible AI

AWS describes responsible AI through core dimensions:

- **Fairness**: weighing how the system affects different groups of stakeholders.
- **Explainability**: understanding and evaluating what the system outputs.
- **Privacy and security**: obtaining, using and protecting data and models appropriately.
- **Safety**: preventing harmful outputs and misuse.
- **Controllability**: being able to monitor and steer system behavior.
- **Veracity and robustness**: producing correct outputs, even with unexpected or adversarial inputs.
- **Governance**: applying best practices across the AI supply chain.
- **Transparency**: helping stakeholders make informed choices about using the system.

Inclusivity (serving all user groups) runs through these and is tested alongside fairness.

### Tools: Amazon Bedrock Guardrails

Amazon Bedrock Guardrails adds configurable safeguards to GenAI applications. It evaluates user inputs and model responses and can be applied to foundation model calls or used on its own through the `ApplyGuardrail` API. Its safeguards include:

- **Content filters** for harmful categories such as hate, insults, sexual content, violence, misconduct and prompt attacks.
- **Denied topics** that block subjects you define as off limits.
- **Word filters** that block custom words or phrases, such as profanity or competitor names.
- **Sensitive information filters** that block or mask PII, including custom regex patterns.
- **Contextual grounding checks** that detect hallucinations, meaning responses not grounded in the source or irrelevant to the query. They are common in RAG applications.
- **Automated Reasoning checks** that validate responses against logical rules.

### Responsible model selection

Choose models with the whole impact in mind. The Generative AI Lens advises asking whether GenAI is needed at all, using right-sized models instead of the largest one, and using elastic or serverless capacity so resources are not idle. Smaller, efficient models reduce energy use and cost.

### Legal and business risks of GenAI

- Intellectual property infringement claims when outputs or training data resemble protected works.
- Biased model outputs that can lead to discrimination complaints.
- Loss of customer trust after harmful or embarrassing outputs.
- End user risk, such as people acting on wrong advice.
- Hallucinations, which are confident but false outputs.

### Datasets, bias and variance

Good datasets are inclusive, diverse, balanced and drawn from curated, vetted sources. Data that under-represents a group produces models that perform worse for that group. Label quality matters too, because biased or sloppy labels teach the model the same errors.

- **High bias** means the model is too simple (underfitting). It performs poorly on training and new data.
- **High variance** means the model is too sensitive to the training data (overfitting). It performs well on training data but poorly on new data.

Either problem can produce inaccurate results, and unequal accuracy across demographic groups is a fairness problem.

### Detecting and monitoring bias and truthfulness

- **Analyze label quality** in the training data.
- **Subgroup analysis**: compare metrics for each demographic group, not just overall.
- **Human audits**: reviewers sample outputs for trustworthiness and truthfulness.
- **Amazon SageMaker Clarify** detects bias in data and models and explains predictions with feature attributions.

## Key terms

| Term              | Meaning                                                    |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hallucination     | A fluent output that is false or unsupported by the source |
| Overfitting       | Memorizing training data; poor on new data (high variance) |
| Underfitting      | Model too simple; poor on all data (high bias)             |
| Subgroup analysis | Measuring performance separately per group                 |
| Grounding         | Tying a response to a trusted source                       |

## Comparisons

| Need                               | Use                                               |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Block harmful or off-topic content | Bedrock Guardrails content filters, denied topics |
| Mask PII in outputs                | Guardrails sensitive information filters          |
| Catch unsupported answers in RAG   | Guardrails contextual grounding check             |
| Measure dataset or model bias      | Amazon SageMaker Clarify                          |
| Judge nuanced truthfulness         | Human audits                                      |

## Exam tips

- Match the risk to the control: PII points to sensitive information filters, hallucinations to grounding checks, off-limits subjects to denied topics.
- Poor training and test performance means underfitting; great training but poor test performance means overfitting.
- A single overall accuracy number can hide unfair results; subgroup analysis exposes them.
- Responsible model selection favors right-sized, efficient models over the biggest one.
