# Recognize the importance of transparent and explainable models

This task covers why people need to understand how AI models reach their outputs, how to tell transparent models from opaque ones, which AWS tools and documents help, and how to design AI that people can question and trust. On the exam, expect scenario questions that ask you to match a transparency need to the right tool or practice.

## Key concepts

### Transparent versus opaque models

A **transparent** model discloses how it was built: its architecture, training data sources, evaluation results, limitations, and license. An **explainable** model lets people understand why it produced a given output, for example which input features mattered most. **Interpretability** is the direct readability of a model's inner logic, as with linear regression or a small decision tree. Large deep neural networks and many foundation models are **black boxes**: they can be accurate, but their reasoning is hard to trace without extra tools.

### Tools and documents for transparency

- **Amazon SageMaker Model Cards** record a model's intended uses, risk rating, training details, and evaluation results in one place for reviewers and auditors.
- **Amazon SageMaker Clarify** explains predictions with feature attributions and also detects bias in data and models.
- **Amazon Bedrock Model Evaluations** compares foundation models on metrics such as accuracy, robustness, and toxicity, using automatic metrics, human reviewers, or a judge model.
- **Open source models**, together with published **data documentation** and **licensing**, let teams inspect what they adopt. The license states permitted uses, so read it before commercial use.

### Tradeoffs: safety, transparency, interpretability, and performance

More complex models often perform better but are harder to interpret. Simpler models are easier to explain but may be less accurate. High-stakes or regulated decisions, such as lending, favor interpretability. Disclosure also has limits: revealing every detail of a model or its safeguards can help attackers, so organizations balance openness with safety. Measure both interpretability and performance, then choose a balance that fits the risk.

### Human-centered design for explainable AI

Keep people informed and in control:

- Tell users when AI is involved and label AI-generated content.
- Show plain-language reasons, confidence, and limitations.
- Provide **user-feedback mechanisms** such as ratings and report buttons.
- Keep human oversight, including the ability to override or appeal.

## Key terms

| Term                | Meaning                                                                |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Transparency        | Openness about how a model was built, trained, evaluated, and licensed |
| Explainability      | Ability to describe why a model produced a specific output             |
| Interpretability    | How directly a person can understand a model's inner workings          |
| Black box           | A model whose reasoning is hard for humans to follow                   |
| Feature attribution | A measure of how much each input contributed to a prediction           |
| Model card          | A structured document of a model's purpose, data, risks, and results   |
| Human-in-the-loop   | People review, override, or approve AI decisions                       |

## Comparisons

| Need                             | Best fit                             |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Document a model for governance  | SageMaker Model Cards                |
| Explain a single prediction      | SageMaker Clarify                    |
| Compare foundation models        | Bedrock Model Evaluations            |
| Inspect weights and data notes   | Open source model with clear license |
| Collect user opinions on outputs | Feedback controls in the application |

Model cards describe a model as a whole. Clarify explains individual decisions. Neither replaces the other.

## Exam tips

- "Document intended use, risk, and evaluation results" points to Model Cards.
- "Which features drove this prediction" points to SageMaker Clarify.
- "Compare models or use human reviewers" points to Bedrock Model Evaluations.
- When regulators demand explanations, an interpretable model or explanation tool beats a slightly more accurate black box.
- Do not confuse documentation tools (Model Cards) with logging tools (AWS CloudTrail, Amazon CloudWatch).
- Human-centered answers keep people informed and in control; avoid options that hide AI use or remove review.
